## Autonomous Systems

The internet is divided into **autonomous systems** (routing domains) each of which is under the
control of (and corresponds to) a single administrative entity (University, company, backbone network etc.). 
- Neighbouring systems will interact to coordinate routing and have business relationships.
- They can be identified by 32 bit values called **AS Numbers**

**Inter-domain Routing**: A network with two autonomous systems.
### AS Topology

- **Node:** Autonomous System
	- There is a high variability in node degree ("power law") as there are only a few very highly-connected AS's while most only have a few connections
- **Edge:** Two AS’s that connect to each other

AS topology can be inferred by:
- Mapping `traceroute` data to the AS level
- Measurements of the inter-domain routing protocol

These approaches are hard because data is often out of date and data collection is also difficult.
- Seeing peer-peer edges is difficult.

#### Business Relations within AS'

**Customer Provider**

- Customer needs to be reachable from everyone 
	- The job of the provider
- Customer does not want to provide a transit service
	- Traffic in but no traffic out

**Peer-Peer Relationship**

Peers exchange traffic between customers.
- AS exports only customer routes to a peer
- AS exports a peer’s routes only to its customers

**Backbone networks** Have multiple Points-of-Presence (PoPs) with lots of communication between PoPs. They accommodate traffic demands and limit delay.
- *Inter-PoP links* are long distance and high-bandwidth
- *Intra-PoP links* are shorter cables that have aggregated bandwidth
## Route Propagation

Provide an additional way to hierarchically aggregate routing information is a large internet. Divide the routing problem in two parts:
1. Routing within a single autonomous system (selected by an AS)
2. Routing between autonomous systems (an internet-wide standard)
### EGP and BGP: Inter-domain Routing Protocols

**Exterior Gateway Protocol (EGP)**
- Forced a tree-like topology onto the Internet
- Did not allow for the topology to become general
- Tree like structure: there is a single backbone and autonomous systems are connected only as parents and children and not as peers

**Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)**

- Assumes that the Internet is an arbitrarily interconnected set of ASs. 
- Today’s Internet consists of an interconnection of multiple backbone networks (they are usually called service provider networks, and they are operated by private companies rather than the government)
- Sites are connected to each other in arbitrary ways
